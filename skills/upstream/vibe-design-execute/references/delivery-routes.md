# Delivery route selection

Choose a route after the feature spec and structural direction are sufficiently clear.

## Direct rendered prototype

Prefer when:

- approved tokens, components, and templates cover the work;
- interaction and state behavior are the main uncertainty;
- realistic browser/device evidence is needed quickly;
- the requested deliverable is a working prototype or implementation.

Risks:

- premature implementation can freeze weak structure;
- local code choices can bypass design-system gaps;
- visual refinement can be limited by the chosen runtime.

## Professional design-tool route

Prefer when:

- the requested deliverable is a native design file;
- visual composition, typography, illustration, spatial behavior, or novel interaction needs focused exploration;
- implementation constraints are known but should not dominate early visual reasoning;
- stakeholders need editable design artifacts before code.

Risks:

- interaction and real-content behavior can remain hypothetical;
- tokens/components may drift from implementation;
- a polished static frame can conceal missing states.

## Hybrid route

Prefer when both interaction evidence and high visual craft are material:

1. establish structure and primary behavior in a runnable or inspectable artifact;
2. refine difficult visual decisions in the design surface;
3. record approved decisions in foundation/feature documents;
4. synchronize the implementation/prototype;
5. evaluate the final source of truth, not a stale preview.

## Route decision record

Record:

- selected route and why;
- requested final deliverable;
- source of truth at each phase;
- sync owner and method;
- known fidelity limitations;
- evaluation surface.
